Emergency & Routine Commercial HVAC Repairs FAQs

How soon should emergency commercial HVAC repairs address a Chicago storefront with no heat?

A store or office with no heat loses revenue by the hour. In January a dead system also threatens pipes on an exterior wall. City & Suburban offers same-day service when the schedule allows and emergency HVAC service every day till midnight, except Sundays; maintenance plan members get priority dispatch. The Chicago Heat Ordinance sets minimums for dwelling units, not a blanket temperature for commercial-only space. Where the same system also heats apartments above the shop and the landlord supplies that heat, those units must reach 68 degrees from 8:30 a.m. to 10:30 p.m. and 66 degrees overnight from September 15 to June 1 (Chicago Municipal Code 13-196-410), subject to the code's exceptions. That puts a legal minimum on the residential space.

What can a manager safely check before a repair visit?

A gas smell, smoke or a carbon monoxide alarm comes first: everyone leaves the building and calls 911, or the gas utility for a gas odor, from outside. Otherwise, four checks are reasonable for staff. Confirm the thermostat is calling for heat or cool, not fan only. Look for a tripped breaker. Check whether a service switch near the furnace or air handler was flipped off. Where the filter sits in an accessible indoor slot, shut the system off as the manufacturer directs before checking whether it is packed with dust. Anything past that is technician work: opening energized or gas-fired equipment, repeatedly resetting a locked-out control, or going onto a roof. City & Suburban diagnoses before quoting, and those answers give the technician a head start.

How is a rooftop unit's real fault identified?

City & Suburban diagnoses before quoting, so a repair is priced against a named failure instead of a guess. One symptom hides many causes. Warm air at the registers can mean a dead compressor, a tripped high-pressure switch, a blocked condenser or a failed contactor. A real diagnosis reads the fault history and control sequence first, then measures refrigerant pressure, voltage and amp draw before a part comes off. Parts replaced on a hunch get paid for twice. A diagnosis built on those readings keeps the repair tied to the failure rather than to a brand.
