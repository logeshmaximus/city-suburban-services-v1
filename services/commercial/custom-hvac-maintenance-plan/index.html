How should filter schedules be discussed for commercial equipment?

Filter intervals follow load, not the calendar. A one inch filter in a hard-working rooftop unit can load up inside a month. A four inch media filter in a quiet office may hold for a season. Kitchens, salons, gyms and print rooms load filters faster than desk space does. A clogged air filter restricts airflow, reducing capacity, freezing coils in summer and tripping limit switches in winter. City & Suburban offers seasonal check-ups. Discuss filter schedules, supply and responsibility between visits in the custom proposal rather than assuming those tasks are included.

What safety checks belong in the scope discussion for gas-fired equipment?

Gas heating sections and boilers carry combustion risks, especially where commercial equipment goes unseen for weeks. Cracked heat exchangers, blocked flues, failing pressure switches and dirty burners can show up as combustion problems before comfort problems. If a carbon monoxide alarm sounds or anyone smells gas, everyone should leave the building first. Call 911, or the gas utility for a gas odor, from outside. Illinois has a carbon monoxide alarm law for dwellings; needs for other spaces depend on the building. City & Suburban sends NATE-certified technicians. Ask which safety checks the plan includes and how a finding such as a cracked heat exchanger would be reported.
