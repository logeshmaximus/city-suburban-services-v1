Heater installation questions

How is furnace size decided for heater installation in a Chicago home?

Heater installation sizing starts with the building, not the nameplate on the old unit. Square footage, ceiling height, window area and insulation all matter. So does the capacity of the existing ducts. Many Chicago houses were built for leakier walls and single-pane glass. A like-for-like swap in one of those homes often lands oversized. An oversized furnace short cycles, heats rooms unevenly and works its blower harder than it should. A proposal should state the capacity selected and the building details behind it, so the homeowner can check the basis rather than the nameplate.

When should a Chicago homeowner plan a replacement as a furnace ages?

Service life is a range, not a date. A gas furnace generally gives 15 to 20 years. A steam or hot-water boiler often runs 25 years or longer, a heat pump or ductless system 12 to 15 years, and a thermostat about 10 years. Chicago shortens the furnace end of that range. A long heating season means high run hours, and a cabinet squeezed into a tight closet with restricted return air works harder for the same heat. Short cycling from an oversized unit adds wear on the blower and the ignition parts. Condition and service history decide more than the date stamp. Jessica F. put it plainly in November 2025: "Furnace is nearing its useful life but not yet!"

What furnace efficiency makes sense for an older Chicago house?

Two classes dominate. Mid-efficiency furnaces rated near 80% AFUE vent through a chimney or a metal flue. Condensing furnaces at 95% to 98% AFUE vent through plastic pipe. Those also drain acidic condensate to a floor drain or a small pump. The higher class burns less gas across a long Chicago heating season. The saving is biggest in a drafty building with high run hours. The catch sits in heater installation. A condensing furnace needs a vent route, a condensate path, and a plan for any gas appliance left on the old chimney. Where that route is genuinely impossible, an 80% unit is the honest answer. Efficiency programs belong in the same conversation: which utility or federal incentives apply to the proposed model, and who files the paperwork. Terms change, so each should be confirmed before equipment is ordered.

What do single-stage, two-stage and modulating furnaces change?

Those labels describe how the burner runs. A single-stage furnace fires at full output every time the thermostat calls, so rooms swing warm and then cool. A two-stage unit spends most of a Chicago winter on the lower stage, which lengthens cycles and evens out room temperatures. A modulating furnace adjusts output in small increments and holds the steadiest temperature. A variable-speed blower is a separate choice, and it moves air more quietly at low speed. Staging raises equipment cost and can widen the control and wiring scope, so it belongs in the written quote rather than a verbal upsell. Kathy G., September 2025: "I had a 2-stage furnace and thermostat installed by Eddie and Mike in my condo's custom utility closet."

Why does the chimney come up during high-efficiency furnace installation?

A 90%+ furnace pulls most of the heat out of the flue gas. Its exhaust leaves cool and wet, so it vents through plastic pipe instead of masonry. Taking the old furnace off a shared chimney leaves that flue serving one remaining gas appliance. The remaining appliance may not produce enough heat to keep a tall masonry chimney warm, and the flue may now be oversized for it. Cool flue gas can then condense inside the chimney. That can damage the liner over years and can push combustion gases back into the building. The fix is a design decision: resize or line the flue, or re-route the remaining appliance. It belongs in the plan before the new furnace is ordered.

Heater installation and replacement in the City of Chicago is generally permitted work, and the written quote should say who pulls the permit. The permit record also shows how routine the job is. Declared value is the number a contractor files, not a quote, so read it as scale rather than price. The permit matters for a second reason. The record follows the building and can surface when the property is sold or refinanced.

What happens on heater installation day, and how long does the work take?

A straightforward furnace installation in a Chicago home is often a one-day job, and the written quote should state the expected schedule. A typical scope shuts off gas and power, then disconnects and removes the old unit. The new cabinet is set, the plenum and gas line adapted, the vent run, condensate piped where the equipment produces it, and the controls wired. Then the system is fired, with combustion and airflow checked before the crew leaves. Expect more time when the vent route changes, when chimney work is needed, or when a closet must be rebuilt. Which Chicago buildings are a poor fit for a straight furnace installation?

Three cases stand out. A building heated by steam or hot water usually has no duct system for a furnace to feed, so its replacement options start with boiler services. A home with no ducts at all may suit wall-mounted ductless equipment; adding a full duct run to an older frame building depends on construction, access and wall and ceiling restoration, which a quote should spell out. A system only a few years old with one failed part may only need a repair, which belongs with furnace repair. Age, running cost, the diagnosed condition and the count of recent failures help decide which group a house falls into.

What safety work belongs in a gas heater installation?

Combustion safety is the part a homeowner cannot inspect. A new gas furnace needs adequate combustion air for the room it sits in. The vent has to be correctly sized and sealed, and the gas line sized for the input rating. The burner and the draft then get verified with the unit running under load. Illinois requires carbon monoxide alarms in dwellings with fuel-burning appliances. That rule sits in the Carbon Monoxide Alarm Detector Act (430 ILCS 135), and an install is the moment to confirm the alarms work. City & Suburban sends NATE-certified technicians, a certification that tests combustion and airflow knowledge rather than product selling.
