When the heater stops doing its job

A Chicago condo can lose heat with the furnace still making noise. A house can feel cold even while air moves through the vents. The observations below help separate a repair request from an emergency and give the technician a useful starting point.

Furnace will not start Call today Diagnosis first

Likely causes

  • Thermostat is not calling for heat
  • Loss of electrical power to the furnace
  • Ignition fault or safety lockout

Check safely first

  • With no odor or alarm, check the thermostat is set to heat above room temperature.
  • Note whether the furnace is silent or tries to start.
  • Keep panels closed; avoid repeated resets or attempts to light the furnace.

What the technician does

The technician follows the start sequence to find where operation stops. A control or power problem differs from a failed part or safety shutdown. The technician identifies the failed stage and explains the proposed repair before a price is approved.

Age alone does not decide the outcome. Compare the diagnosed repair cost and existing heater condition with furnace replacement before choosing another repair.

Why do two furnace repairs on the same block cost different amounts?

The part decides most of it. An igniter or a flame sensor is a small, stocked item. A blower motor, an inducer assembly or a control board costs more and takes longer to fit. Access matters too: a furnace in a tight attic or a closet behind plumbing takes more time than one standing open in a basement. Age changes both the price and the supply of parts, and a system still inside its parts warranty changes the math again. Which checks are safe to make at home before calling for furnace repair?

With a gas smell, smoke or a CO alarm, skip all checks: leave and call 911 or the gas utility from outside. Otherwise, a few checks are safe. Confirm the thermostat is set to heat and has working batteries. Find the furnace service switch, which looks like a light switch and is easy to flip by mistake. When is a heater fault a safety emergency rather than a repair call?

A gas smell, a burning smell that does not clear, soot around the cabinet, or a sounding carbon monoxide alarm is not a service booking. The step is to leave the building, then call the gas utility and 911 from outside. Illinois requires carbon monoxide alarms near the sleeping areas of a home, under the Carbon Monoxide Alarm Detector Act. A working carbon monoxide alarm is a required last line of defense, not a test for a cracked heat exchanger. The crack is found by inspecting the heat exchanger and testing combustion during a heating cycle.
