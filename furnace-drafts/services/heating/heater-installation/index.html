1 Furnace size for the houseCapacity should match the home's heat loss. A larger furnace costs more, and an oversized one short-cycles and wears faster.

How should sizing work in a new furnace installation quote?

Sizing should start from the house, not from the label on the old furnace. A common shortcut copies the old capacity, yet many Chicago homes have gained insulation, new windows or a finished basement since the last install. An oversized furnace short-cycles, wears faster and leaves rooms uneven. An undersized one runs nonstop in a cold snap. A sound quote rests on a heat-loss calculation for the home, often called a Manual J. It also checks that the ducts can carry the airflow. What do AFUE ratings mean for gas furnace installation?

AFUE stands for annual fuel utilization efficiency. It is the share of a season's gas that ends up as heat in the house. At 80% AFUE, a fifth of the fuel leaves as hot exhaust up the chimney. Condensing furnaces rated 90% or higher, in classes such as 95%, 96%, 97% and 98%, cool the exhaust enough to reclaim more heat. That design vents through plastic pipe and needs a drain for the water it makes. Over a long Chicago winter, the higher class costs more to install and less to run. The better fit depends on how long the household plans to stay and whether a side-wall vent route is practical. City & Suburban puts the full price in writing for both an 80% and a 90%+ option. The cost gap is then on paper before work starts.

Single-stage, two-stage or modulating: which furnace suits a Chicago home?

A single-stage furnace runs at full fire until the thermostat is satisfied, then shuts off. In a two-flat or a tall greystone, that on-off pattern can leave the top floor swinging between warm and cool. Two-stage and modulating furnaces run on a low setting for most of the winter and step up only in deep cold. Many pair with a variable-speed blower. The result is quieter running and more even rooms, at a higher price than single-stage. City & Suburban installs American Standard equipment and puts the full price in writing before work starts. Kathy G. (Sep 2025) wrote: "I had a 2-stage furnace and thermostat installed by Eddie and Mike in my condo's custom utility closet."

What venting changes can a new furnace need in a bungalow, two-flat or greystone?

Many older Chicago homes have masonry chimneys built for far older heating. Cook County Assessor data puts the median year built in Jefferson township, the bungalow belt, at 1927. In West Chicago township, 83.0% of residential properties predate 1940. An 80% furnace on an oversized, unlined flue can cool its exhaust until moisture damages the masonry. A liner is often part of the job. A 90%+ furnace usually vents through a side wall instead, which can leave another gas appliance alone on an oversized flue. What must a Chicago landlord plan for when a rental furnace needs replacing?

The Chicago Heat Ordinance (Municipal Code 13-196-410) applies where the landlord supplies heat. From September 15 to June 1, the general rule is at least 68°F from 8:30 a.m. to 10:30 p.m. and 66°F overnight. A furnace that dies in a two-flat during that window is a compliance problem as well as a comfort one. Replacing an aging furnace before the season avoids a rushed January job. Is carbon monoxide a concern when an old furnace is replaced?

Any change to a gas furnace or its vent deserves a carbon monoxide check. An old furnace with a cracked heat exchanger or a blocked flue can leak exhaust into the house. A new furnace on a mismatched vent can do the same. The Illinois Carbon Monoxide Alarm Detector Act requires CO alarms in homes with fuel-burning appliances.
