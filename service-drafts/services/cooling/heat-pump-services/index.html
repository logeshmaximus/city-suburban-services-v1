Outdoor unit stays coated in ice Call today Usually one visit

Likely causes

  • A defrost control, sensor or reversing valve that no longer starts or ends defrost
  • Roof or gutter water dripping onto the unit and refreezing
  • Low refrigerant charge from a leak, which makes the coil run too cold

Check safely first

  • Watch for 90 minutes: a normal defrost clears frost within about 10 minutes
  • Look above the unit for a gutter or downspout dripping onto it

What the technician does

The technician forces a defrost cycle and watches it start, run and end. Then the defrost sensor and board get tested , the reversing valve shift is confirmed, and the charge is checked. Ice from a roof drip is a gutter fix, not a parts sale.

Service, repair or replace?

When a tune-up finds more, the $5,000 rule for heat pumps

Some heat pump service visits find a failing part. Multiply the heat pump's age by the repair price. A result over $5,000 means a replacement price deserves a look beside the repair quote; under it, repair usually wins. Heat pumps installed before 2010 usually run on R-22, and US production and imports of R-22 ended in 2020. A refrigerant leak on one of these weighs heavily toward replacement. A newer heat pump with one failed capacitor is worth fixing, and City & Suburban repairs first where repair is right.

What does a heat pump maintenance service include?

A sound heat pump maintenance service checks the whole system in both modes. Outside, that means cleaning the coil, straightening bent fins, testing the fan motor and checking the base pan drains. Inside, the visit checks the filter, blower, indoor coil and drain line. How often should a heat pump be serviced: twice a year or once?

Twice a year suits most Chicago heat pumps. An air conditioner works only in summer, but a heat pump heats from October to April and cools from June to September. That is roughly double the run hours, so wear builds twice as fast. Many heat pumps still get an air-conditioner tune-up once a year, in spring. That visit never tests defrost, the reversing valve or backup heat, the parts that fail in a January cold snap. A spring visit readies the cooling side and cleans the coil after winter. A fall visit tests defrost, backup heat and the reversing valve before the first freeze. If only one visit fits, keep the fall one. What does a spring heat pump tune-up check before cooling season?

Spring service gets the heat pump ready for months of cooling. Winter leaves salt, grit and leaves in the outdoor coil, and late spring adds cottonwood fluff that mats on the fins. Why does a heat pump ice up in winter, and when is the ice a problem?

In heat mode, the outdoor coil runs colder than the air. Moisture freezes on it, most often in damp weather near freezing. The defrost cycle reverses the heat pump briefly, warms the coil and melts the frost, often with a whoosh and a cloud of steam. That is normal. A unit encased in ice for hours is not. Common causes are a failed defrost sensor or board, a stuck reversing valve, low refrigerant or roof water dripping onto the unit. Forcing a defrost cycle on the visit shows which part has failed. City & Suburban answers emergency calls 24 hours a day, 7 days a week, when a heat pump stops heating.

How should snow around an outdoor heat pump be handled in a Chicago winter?

A heat pump needs open air on every side to heat the home. Snow drifted against the coil, or shoveled from a gangway onto the unit, blocks that air. Brush loose snow away with a broom and keep about 2 feet clear on every side. Never chip ice with a shovel or a pick, because the coil fins and refrigerant tubing are thin. If ice will not clear, switch the thermostat to Emergency Heat and call. After heavy snow, a technician checks for crushed fins and notes where a raised stand would help. The City & Suburban emergency line runs 24/7 for a unit that will not come back.

Why is the AUX heat light on, and should the backup heat be checked?

AUX means the backup heat is helping the heat pump. That is normal on the coldest nights and during a defrost. A light that stays on in mild weather points to a heat pump that is not keeping up. EM or Emergency Heat is different: it shuts the heat pump off and runs only the backup, which costs more to run. A tune-up measures each heat strip and checks the sequencers and the outdoor temperature lockout. The goal is a heat pump that carries the load, with backup heat only helping. Does heat pump service and repair include refrigerant and electrical checks?

It should. A heat pump moves heat with refrigerant, so the technician measures pressures and temperatures in the mode the weather allows. A charge that reads low means a leak somewhere, and topping it up without finding the leak only buys time. On the electrical side, the technician tightens connections, tests the run capacitor and contactor, and measures amp draw on the compressor and fan motors. How often should a heat pump filter be changed?

Check the filter monthly during peak heating and cooling months, and change it when it looks gray. A heat pump moves a lot of air at a gentle temperature, so it depends on airflow more than a furnace does. A clogged filter can trip backup heat in winter and freeze the indoor coil in summer. Thin filters often last one to two months. On a service visit, the technician checks the filter, blower and supply temperature together. Which thermostat settings suit a heat pump?

A steady setting usually suits a heat pump. Deep overnight setbacks force a big recovery in the morning, and many thermostats call backup heat to make it up. Small setbacks of two or three degrees, or a thermostat with heat pump recovery, avoid that. The thermostat must also be set up as a heat pump, with the reversing valve wired correctly, or heating and cooling can run backward. Emergency Heat should stay off unless the outdoor unit has failed. Thermostat setup and staging get checked on every heat pump tune-up. Does regular heat pump service matter for the warranty?

It can. Many manufacturer warranties require registration after installation, and a claim can be refused when a failure traces to neglect, such as a matted outdoor coil or a clogged drain. Terms vary by brand and model, so read the warranty papers or the manufacturer's site for the exact rules. Keep dated service records that list the coil cleaning, refrigerant readings and the defrost test from each visit. A receipt with only a date proves less.
