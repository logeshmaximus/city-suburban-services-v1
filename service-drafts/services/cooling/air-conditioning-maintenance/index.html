  • •Swap the filter if it looks gray or packed with dust, and note its size for the technician.
  • •Clear leaves, cottonwood fluff and stored items from around the outdoor unit, leaving about 2 feet of open space.
  • •Set the thermostat to Cool, a few degrees below room temperature, and replace its batteries if the display is blank.

Cooling upkeep questions, answered

What does an air conditioner maintenance visit include?

A sound visit works through both halves of the system. Outside, the technician shuts off power, cleans the condenser coil, checks the fan motor and tests the capacitor and contactor. Inside, the evaporator coil, drain pan and drain line are inspected and cleared, and the filter is checked. With the system running, refrigerant pressures, the temperature drop across the coil and thermostat accuracy are measured. A single split system usually takes about an hour to an hour and a half, while multi-head ductless systems and rooftop units take longer. City & Suburban's NATE-certified technicians service every make, and a good visit ends with each reading explained in plain words.

When should a Chicago home book an AC tune-up?

April and May are the right window in Chicago. The condenser has sat through a winter of snow, road salt and leaves, and late spring adds cottonwood fluff that mats the coil fins. A visit before the first heat wave, often in June, finds a weak part while there is time to fix it calmly. Booking after the first 90 degree day means waiting with every household whose cooling just quit. City & Suburban maintenance plan members get seasonal check-ups, so the spring visit is on the calendar before demand peaks.

How often does an air conditioner need service?

A central air conditioner needs a full service once a year, done in spring before steady use. A home with pets, a nearby construction site or heavy cottonwood may need the outdoor coil rinsed more often. Heat pumps work in both seasons, so many owners book a spring and a fall visit. Filters run on a shorter clock: every 1 to 3 months in the cooling season. A cooling maintenance plan from City & Suburban bundles seasonal check-ups, priority dispatch and member discounts. The yearly service is then not left to memory.

Can one visit handle AC repair and maintenance?

Often, yes. A tune-up that finds a capacitor below its rated value or a pitted contactor has found a repair. A small part like that is commonly replaced on the same visit. A sound visit shows the reading and explains what it means. City & Suburban puts the price in writing first, and nothing is swapped without approval. Bigger findings, such as a refrigerant leak or a failing compressor, need their own diagnosis and become a separate AC repair visit. Does an AC tune-up include a refrigerant top-off?

A sound tune-up checks the refrigerant charge, but a healthy system should not need topping off. Refrigerant travels in a sealed loop, so a low reading means a leak somewhere. Adding refrigerant without finding the leak buys a few weeks or a season, then the cooling fades again. A sound tune-up compares pressures with the manufacturer's chart and reports a low charge. City & Suburban diagnoses the cause before quoting, and the leak search and air conditioner repair are then priced in writing. Older systems that use R-22, a refrigerant no longer produced, make that repair decision harder.

Why does the outdoor condenser need cleaning every spring?

The condenser's job is to release the heat pulled out of the house. Its thin aluminum fins clog with cottonwood seed, grass clippings, dryer lint and city grime, and a matted coil cannot shed heat. Pressure inside the system climbs, the compressor works harder and draws more power, and on the hottest afternoon a safety control may shut it down. A proper cleaning shuts off power, rinses from the inside out at low pressure and straightens bent fins. City & Suburban's NATE-certified technicians service every make of outdoor unit, and a sound tune-up treats the condenser cleaning as a required step, not an add-on.

What happens to the drain line during AC maintenance?

An air conditioner pulls gallons of water out of humid summer air, and that water leaves through the drain pan and drain line. Algae and sludge grow in the line, and a clog backs water into the pan, onto the furnace or through a condo ceiling. A sound visit flushes the line, checks the trap and confirms the pan is intact. It also tests the float switch that shuts the system off before an overflow. In a high-rise condo, that small switch can be the difference between a service call and a ceiling repair in the unit below.

What should a homeowner do between AC service visits?

Most of the care between visits takes a few minutes a month. Check the filter monthly in summer and replace it when it looks gray. Keep about 2 feet clear around the outdoor unit, and aim mower discharge and dryer vents away from it. With the power off, hose loose debris off the fins gently from the outside, and never use a pressure washer. Keep supply registers open, since closing off rooms starves the indoor coil of airflow. Leave refrigerant, wiring and the drain pan to a trained technician.

Are ductless mini-splits and heat pumps maintained the same way?

Not quite. Each type needs its own steps. A ductless mini-split has a washable filter and a small coil in every wall head. Each head also has a thin drain line that clogs easily, so each one is cleaned and checked on its own. A heat pump cools exactly like an air conditioner in summer, then reverses to heat in winter. It needs the spring checks plus a fall visit for the heating side. City & Suburban services both types, every make, and heat pump maintenance covers the winter side in more depth.

Two-flats often keep condensers on the roof or a back porch. A sound visit plans for access: roof keys, association rules and a clear path to the closet. Does skipping AC maintenance affect the warranty?

It can. Many manufacturer warranties exclude failures caused by neglect, such as a compressor that overheated behind a clogged coil or water damage from a blocked drain. The exact terms differ by brand and model, so the homeowner should read the warranty papers and any registration rules. A dated service record from each visit is the simplest proof of care.
