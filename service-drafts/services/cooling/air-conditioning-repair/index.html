AC runs, but warm air comes from the vents Call today Often a single-part fix

Likely causes

  • Thermostat set to Heat, or the fan set to On instead of Auto
  • Outdoor unit stopped by a tripped breaker or a failed capacitor
  • Refrigerant running low because of a leak

Check safely first

  • Set the thermostat to Cool and the fan to Auto
  • Look outside: if the outdoor fan is still, check the breaker once

What the technician does

The technician confirms the thermostat is calling for cooling and checks that the compressor and outdoor fan run. A capacitor reading exposes an electrical fault. Gauge pressures and the temperature drop across the coil reveal a low charge, which points to a leak somewhere in the system.

Outdoor unit hums, but the fan blade sits still Call today Common, widely stocked parts

Likely causes

  • A weak or failed run capacitor
  • A seized condenser fan motor
  • Pitted contactor points that pass too little power

Check safely first

  • Turn the system off at the thermostat so the compressor does not overheat
  • Never push the blade or reach through the fan guard

What the technician does

A capacitor holds a charge even with the power off, so the technician discharges it before testing it against its label rating. The fan motor and contactor are tested next, since pitted contacts can make a new part fail early. These parts are common and widely stocked, so the fix is often quick once the cause is confirmed.

Ice on the copper line or the indoor coil Call today Thaw first: several hours

Likely causes

Ice is a symptom, so the coil is thawed before pressures are read; frozen readings mislead. Airflow and the blower are checked. When airflow is good and the charge is low, a sound repair looks for the leak. Pricing

What sets the price of an air conditioner repair

City & Suburban does not post a price list for AC repair, because the failed part, the refrigerant work and the access decide the cost. Two systems with the same symptom can need very different repairs. These six factors move the price, and the exact figure goes in writing for approval before any work starts.

1 Which part failedA capacitor or contactor is a small part and a short job. A condenser fan motor, a blower motor or a compressor costs far more in parts and labor.
2 Which refrigerant the system usesSystems installed before 2010 usually run on R-22, which is no longer made, so every pound comes from reclaimed stock. Newer R-410A systems are easier to serve. The refrigerant type is on the outdoor unit's label.
3 Where the equipment sitsA condenser in a side yard is quick to reach. Equipment on the roof of a three-flat, or an air handler in a tight attic, takes longer to work on.
4 Leak repair: valve, line or coilFinding the leak is part of the job. A leak at a service valve or flare joint is a small fix; a leak inside the evaporator or outdoor coil can mean a new coil.
5 Parts in stock or orderedCapacitors and contactors are widely stocked. Model-specific motors, coils and control boards may need ordering, which adds a return trip.
6 What the diagnosis turns upA second problem found during the visit, such as a clogged drain or a burned wire, is shown to the homeowner and priced before any work.

Repair or replace?

The $5,000 rule for an aging air conditioner

Multiply the air conditioner's age by the repair price. Above $5,000, a replacement price deserves a place beside the repair quote; below it, fixing the system usually makes sense. The rule only opens the conversation. A ten-year-old system with a dead capacitor is worth fixing almost every time. A failed compressor or a leaking evaporator coil on a system near the end of its life shifts the math. City & Suburban diagnoses first, repairs where repair is right, and recommends replacement only when the evidence supports it.

What counts as an emergency AC repair?

A few situations justify an emergency call. One is no cooling in a heat wave with an infant, an older adult or someone frail at home. Another is a burning smell, sparking at the outdoor unit or a breaker that keeps tripping. Water dripping through a ceiling from an attic air handler counts too. Smoke or flame goes to 911 first. Why is the AC running but blowing warm air?

Warm air with the indoor blower running usually means the outdoor unit has stopped. Common causes are a failed capacitor, a burned contactor or a tripped breaker. A low charge from a refrigerant leak comes next. A thermostat set to Heat is the easiest cause to rule out. A sound diagnosis checks voltage, capacitor values and gauge pressures before naming a part. City & Suburban diagnoses before quoting, so the written price covers the real fault. Noah W. wrote in a Jul 2026 Google review: "Today, Eddie showed up when expected and fixed our AC within 45 minutes."

Why does ice form on the AC line or the indoor coil?

Ice forms when the evaporator coil runs colder than freezing. The usual causes are too little air across the coil, from a clogged filter or a weak blower, or a low charge from a leak. Running a frozen system can send liquid refrigerant to the compressor and damage it. Set the thermostat to Off and the fan to On until the ice melts. A sound repair checks airflow first, then the charge, and looks for the leak. City & Suburban diagnoses before quoting, and a yearly AC tune-up keeps the coil clean.

How does AC leak repair work?

Refrigerant does not get used up like fuel. A system that runs low has a leak, so a sound repair checks the service valves, joints and line set, then the coils. Small leaks at a valve or joint are often fixable on site. On an older system, a recharge without a full leak repair is a short-term option, priced in writing so the homeowner can choose. A leak inside an older evaporator coil can make an AC replacement the better value. City & Suburban diagnoses before quoting. Nisha J. wrote in a Jun 2026 Google review: "Rob investigated the issue carefully and quickly found a refrigerant leak."

Is AC compressor repair possible when the unit will not start?

Often, yes, when the compressor itself is sound. A compressor that hums or clicks may only need a new capacitor, a contactor or a hard start kit, which adds a boost at startup. Shorted or grounded windings are a different story. That calls for a new compressor or a new outdoor unit, and the system's age decides which. A sound diagnosis tests the windings before any quote. Why is water leaking from the air conditioner inside the home?

An air conditioner pulls water out of humid air, and that water drains through a pan and a small line. Algae clogs the line, and the pan overflows. In condos, the air handler often sits in an attic or closet above a finished ceiling, so a clog can stain drywall fast. A float switch shuts the cooling off when water backs up. A sound repair clears the drain line, flushes the pan and tests that switch. City & Suburban puts the price in writing first, and a rusted-through pan is quoted as a separate repair.
