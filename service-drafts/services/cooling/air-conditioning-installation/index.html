Repair or replace: the $5,000 rule for central air

Multiply the air conditioner's age by the repair quote. A result over $5,000 means an AC replacement price belongs beside the repair; under it, repair usually makes sense. A ten-year-old system with a failed capacitor is worth fixing. A failed compressor or a leaking coil on a system near the end of its life is where replacement usually wins. When does AC replacement make more sense than another repair?

Start with the $5,000 rule: multiply the system's age by the repair quote, and above 5,000 a replacement price deserves a look. Then weigh what failed. A bad capacitor or contactor on a ten-year-old system is a routine AC repair. A failed compressor, a leaking coil, or an older system charged with R-22 refrigerant shifts the math toward replacement. How is a central air installation sized for a Chicago home?

Size should come from the house, not from the label on the old unit. A sound quote rests on a cooling load calculation, such as the industry's Manual J method. It weighs floor area, insulation, window size and direction, and how many floors the system serves. Chicago homes test this. A brick bungalow with a finished attic, or the top unit of a two-flat, gains heat through the roof all afternoon. An oversized system cools fast, shuts off, and leaves the air damp. An undersized one runs nonstop in a heat wave. City & Suburban's written quote names the model and capacity, so the size can be compared line by line with another quote.

What does SEER2 mean on a new air conditioner?

SEER2 is the efficiency rating on new central air equipment. The rating belongs to a matched pair, the outdoor condenser plus the indoor coil. Higher tiers often add two-stage or variable-speed compressors, which run longer at low output and pull more moisture from the air. Chicago's cooling season is shorter than in southern states, so the top tier pays back slower here. City & Suburban installs American Standard condensers and coils as matched pairs, so the rated SEER2 applies to the pair that goes in. Nisha J. wrote in a Jun 2026 Google review. "We even asked if we should replace the furnace and upgrade to a two-stage AC, and he gave us honest advice instead of pushing a massive, expensive project."

Why does the indoor coil have to match the furnace?

In most Chicago basements the evaporator coil sits on top of the furnace, and the furnace blower pushes all the cooling air through it. The coil must fit the furnace cabinet, and the blower must move enough air for the system's size. A coil too small for the condenser costs capacity. A blower too weak lets the coil run cold, and it can ice over on humid days. The coil's refrigerant metering device must also suit the new condenser. City & Suburban installs American Standard condensers, coils and furnaces, so the matched parts come from one manufacturer. When an older furnace stays, a sound quote checks its blower before the coil is chosen.

Can the old refrigerant line set be reused with a new system?

Sometimes. The line set is the pair of copper pipes between the outdoor condenser and the indoor coil. Reuse works when the lines are the right size for the new system, free of kinks and leaks, and clean of old oil. Older systems ran on R-22 with mineral oil, while current equipment uses newer refrigerants and oils, so reused lines need flushing or replacing. In a two-flat or greystone, the old lines often run inside finished walls or a chase, and replacing those lines adds labor. City & Suburban's written quote states whether the line set stays, so two quotes can be compared on it.

What happens on AC installation day?

The crew protects floors, shuts off power, and recovers the old refrigerant into a tank instead of releasing it. The old condenser and coil come out, the new coil goes on the furnace, and the condenser goes on its pad or roof stand. The line set is brazed, pressure-tested with nitrogen and pulled into a deep vacuum to remove moisture. Then the condenser's service valves are opened to release its factory refrigerant charge into the lines. Wiring and thermostat are connected, and the system runs while temperatures and pressures are checked. Google reviewers describe City & Suburban crews finishing whole replacements within a day. Can central air go into a home without ductwork?

Yes, with planning. Many older Chicago homes heat with hot water or steam and have no ducts at all. The Cook County Assessor records 98,849 houses and small residential buildings in Chicago's eight townships heated that way. The same data counts 306,261 without central air and 133,080 with it. Two routes are common. An air handler in the attic or a closet can feed short new duct runs, often through ceiling vents on the top floor. A ductless mini-split system skips ducts and mounts a head in each zone. What keeps a new air conditioner running well after installation?

Three habits protect a new system. Register the equipment with the manufacturer soon after installation, because registration can affect the parts warranty. Change or clean the filter on schedule, since a clogged filter starves the coil of air. Keep the outdoor condenser clear of cottonwood fluff, leaves and plantings.
