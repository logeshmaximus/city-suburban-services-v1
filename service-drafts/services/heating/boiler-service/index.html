Likely causes

Pricing

What sets the price of boiler work

No two boiler systems in Chicago are piped the same way, so City & Suburban does not post a flat price for boiler work. The system type, the failed part and the building decide the cost. These six factors move it, and the exact figure is written down and approved before the work begins.

1 Steam or hot waterSteam work centers on water level, vents and pipe pitch. Hot-water work centers on pressure, circulators, zone valves and air removal.
2 The part that failedA clogged low-water cutoff or a stuck vent is a short job. A gas valve, control module or cracked section costs far more in parts and time.
3 Boiler size and radiationA replacement is sized to the radiators it must heat. A bigger load means a bigger boiler, more piping and more labor.
4 Venting and the chimneyOlder boilers vent into masonry chimneys. A new boiler can need a liner or a sidewall vent, and that work belongs in the quote.
5 Access and near-boiler pipingTight two-flat basements, narrow stairs and old piping change the hours. Steam boilers also need correct near-boiler piping to run dry.
6 Age, make and parts supplyParts for discontinued boilers can take time to find. Nights and holidays matter too, since emergency calls run 24/7.

Why are some radiators cold when the boiler is running?

The answer depends on the system. On one-pipe steam, a radiator heats only when its air can escape, so a clogged radiator vent or failed main vent leaves it cold. On two-pipe steam, a failed steam trap is the usual cause. On hot water, trapped air, a weak circulator or a stuck zone valve stops the flow, and low pressure starves the top floor. City & Suburban checks the boiler, then follows the heat to the cold rooms. Vents, valves and pressure are tested before any part is quoted, and the price is written down for approval first.

What causes banging pipes in a steam heating system, and can it be fixed?

Banging, often called water hammer, happens when steam slams into water sitting in a pipe. Common causes are sagging pipes, radiators pitched the wrong way, a boiler filled too high, pressure set too high and dirty water that surges. Most home steam systems were designed to run at very low pressure, often under 2 psi. City & Suburban checks the water line, the pressure control and the pitch of the mains, then fixes the cause instead of the noise. Re-pitching a sagging pipe and skimming a dirty boiler often quiet a system that has banged for years.

What should boiler pressure read, and when is it a problem?

Steam and hot-water boilers read pressure differently. A home steam boiler runs at very low pressure, usually a pound or two. A hot-water boiler stays under pressure all the time. In a two-story or three-story building, 12 to 15 psi when cold is common, rising a little as it heats. A gauge near the relief valve's limit, or a valve that drips, points to a waterlogged expansion tank or a leaking fill valve. City & Suburban tests both, sets the fill pressure for the building's height and never plugs a relief valve to stop a drip.

Is boiler leak repair worth it, or does a leak mean replacement?

Most leaks are worth repairing. Relief valves, fittings, valve packing, circulator flanges and radiator spuds wear out, and those fixes are routine. The leak that changes the answer is a cracked cast-iron section. It shows as water inside the jacket, under the burner or on the floor with no outside source. City & Suburban dries the area, runs the boiler and traces the water before naming a cause. When the boiler itself is cracked, the owner gets a written replacement price beside any patch option, with the trade-offs explained in plain words.

Steam, hot water or combi: which new boiler fits a two-flat or greystone?

The piping decides most of it. A building piped for steam usually stays steam, because converting means new pipe and often new radiators. A hot-water building can take a modulating condensing boiler, which saves fuel when its vent and condensate drain are planned well. A combi boiler suits smaller homes and condos, where one wall-hung unit can also supply domestic hot water. A sound recommendation looks at the radiators, the piping and the chimney before naming a boiler type. City & Suburban puts the price of the chosen option in writing before work starts. What should a boiler installation quote include?

A sound boiler installation quote sizes the new boiler to the radiators, not to the old boiler's label. For steam, that means measuring the connected radiation. For hot water, it means estimating the heat loss of the rooms. The quote should also name the venting plan, any chimney liner, the near-boiler piping and the controls. It should state who pulls the City permit and who removes the old boiler. City & Suburban puts the full price in writing before work starts. Owners comparing bids can ask each contractor how the size was chosen, which quickly separates measured quotes from guesses.

What does an annual boiler service include?

An annual boiler service differs for steam and hot water. A sound steam visit flushes and tests the low-water cutoff and checks the sight glass and pressure control. A hot-water visit checks pressure, the expansion tank, the relief valve and the circulator. Both should end with a combustion and carbon monoxide test. City & Suburban maintenance plans add seasonal check-ups, priority dispatch and member discounts. Marianne Y. wrote in a Sep 2025 Google review: "Rob has been servicing my boiler for several years."

Do radiators need bleeding, and how often?

Only hot-water radiators are bled. Each has a small bleed screw near the top, and opening it with a radiator key lets trapped air out until water appears. Once a season is typical, and more often after the system is drained or refilled. Steam radiators are never bled. On one-pipe steam, each relies on an air vent that should hiss and then close; two-pipe steam radiators use a steam trap instead. City & Suburban checks radiators during boiler service, and a system that needs bleeding every week is tested for the leak behind it.

What does the Chicago Heat Ordinance mean when a building's boiler fails?

The Chicago Heat Ordinance, Municipal Code 13-196-410, covers heat that a landlord supplies. From September 15 to June 1, units must reach 68°F from 8:30 a.m. to 10:30 p.m., and 66°F overnight. The code lists exceptions, but a dead boiler in a two-flat or courtyard building puts the owner on a clock.
