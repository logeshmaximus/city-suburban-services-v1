What the heat pump is doing, and what a repair visit checks

Each card matches a symptom Chicago homeowners report, from ice that never clears to backup heat that never shuts off. Open one for the likely causes, the safe checks to make first and what the technician tests. A burning smell or a breaker that trips twice comes before everything else.

Blowing cool or lukewarm air on heat Call today Usually one visit

Likely causes

  • Thermostat on Cool or Auto, or a wrong O/B setting after a thermostat swap
  • A reversing valve stuck in the cooling position
  • Low refrigerant charge from a leak

Check safely first

  • Confirm the thermostat is set to Heat, not Cool or Auto
  • After ten minutes of running, feel a supply vent: heat pump air feels warm, not hot

What the technician does

The technician measures the air temperature entering and leaving the indoor unit and reads the refrigerant pressures. Those numbers separate a healthy heat pump, which delivers milder air than a furnace, from a stuck reversing valve or a low charge. The thermostat setup is checked too, because one wrong setting can run the system backward.

1 The component that failedA defrost sensor or capacitor is a short repair. A reversing valve or compressor means recovering the refrigerant, brazing and far more labor.

6 Where the outdoor unit sitsA ground pad beside the old outdoor unit is simplest. Two-flat roofs, gangways, alley garages, long refrigerant lines and snow clearance add labor.

Repair or replace?

When a heat pump repair still pays, and when replacement makes more sense

Start with the $5,000 rule: multiply the heat pump's age by the repair quote, and a total over 5,000 means a heat pump replacement price belongs next to the repair. Heat pumps run in summer and winter, so they log more hours than a furnace and wear sooner. A defrost board or fan motor on a younger system is worth fixing. A failed compressor on an older R-22 heat pump rarely is. City & Suburban repairs first where repair is right and writes up both prices when replacement is on the table.

Heat pump repair and installation questions

Can a heat pump heat a Chicago home on below-zero nights?

Yes, with the right equipment and a backup plan. Older heat pumps lost much of the heat output as the outdoor air fell toward zero. Cold-climate models use variable-speed compressors to keep heating on far colder nights. Even so, most Chicago installs keep a backup: electric heat strips in the air handler, or a gas furnace in a dual-fuel setup. A written quote should show the heat pump's rated output at 5°F next to the home's heat loss. City & Suburban installs American Standard heat pumps and puts the exact price in writing before any work starts. A sound quote names the backup heat.

Why does heat pump air feel cooler than furnace air?

A gas furnace sends out very hot air in short bursts. A heat pump delivers milder air over longer run times. On a cold day that air can feel cool on a hand, even while the house holds the thermostat setting. Cool vents and a falling indoor temperature tell a different story, pointing to a stuck reversing valve, a low charge or a wrong thermostat setting. The technician compares the air going into and out of the indoor unit to tell the two apart. City & Suburban technicians are NATE-certified. Is ice on the outdoor unit normal in winter?

A light frost is normal. In heating mode the outdoor coil runs colder than the air, so moisture freezes on the fins. Every so often the heat pump reverses for a few minutes to melt it, often with a whoosh and a cloud of steam. Heavy ice that never clears means the defrost cycle is failing. Common causes are a bad defrost sensor or board, a stopped outdoor fan, low refrigerant or melt water refreezing under the unit. City & Suburban repairs all makes and models. What is the difference between auxiliary heat and emergency heat?

Auxiliary heat switches on by itself. The thermostat calls for it when the heat pump needs help, such as during defrost or a deep cold snap. Emergency heat is a manual setting that shuts the heat pump off and runs only the backup. Both usually run electric heat strips, which cost much more to run than the heat pump. The fix for aux heat that never stops can be a weak outdoor unit or a staging setting. City & Suburban offers upfront pricing with no hidden fees. Can a stuck reversing valve be repaired?

Often, yes. The reversing valve sends refrigerant one way for heating and the other way for cooling. Many failures sit in the electric solenoid coil on the valve, which is a quick part swap. A valve body that sticks needs a bigger heat pump repair. The refrigerant is recovered, a new valve is brazed in, the system is pulled into a vacuum and the charge is weighed back in. On an older system, that cost is weighed against replacement. City & Suburban repairs all makes and models. How should a heat pump contractor size a system for a Chicago house?

Sizing starts with the house, not the label on the old unit. A heat pump installation quote should rest on a heat loss calculation covering walls, windows, insulation, air leaks and the coldest expected night. A brick two-flat and a frame bungalow lose heat very differently. An oversized heat pump short cycles and leaves summer air clammy. An undersized one leans on backup heat all winter. The quote should also list the model's output at low outdoor temperatures. City & Suburban puts the exact price in writing before work starts, and a sound quote lists equipment and backup heat so homeowners can compare quotes line by line.

Can a heat pump work with the existing gas furnace?

Yes. A dual-fuel system pairs a heat pump with a gas furnace. The heat pump heats on mild and moderately cold days, and the furnace takes over below a set outdoor temperature. The same heat pump handles summer cooling, so one outdoor unit does both jobs. Many Chicago homes already have the ducts for this. The Cook County Assessor counts 335,769 Chicago houses and small residential buildings heated by a warm-air furnace. City & Suburban installs American Standard heat pumps paired with matching furnaces, and the installer sets the switchover point for the house. The full story of dual-fuel heating has its own guide.

Does heat pump installation need new ducts or a bigger electrical panel?

Sometimes. A ducted heat pump moves more air than many older furnaces did, so small or leaky ducts can hold it back. The estimate should check duct size and static pressure before the equipment is chosen. All-electric systems with heat strips also draw heavy current, and an older 60 or 100 amp service may need an electrician. The Cook County Assessor counts 98,849 Chicago houses and small residential buildings heated by hot water or steam, with no ducts at all. Those homes often suit a ductless mini-split better. When is heat pump replacement better than another repair?

Age, the failed part and the refrigerant decide most cases. A heat pump runs in summer and winter, so it often wears out sooner than a furnace, commonly at 10 to 15 years. A failed compressor on a heat pump past that age rarely justifies the repair. R-22 systems rely on a refrigerant that is no longer produced. City & Suburban repairs first where repair is right and recommends replacement only when the evidence supports it. To delay that day, a spring and fall check of the coil, defrost controls, charge and filter is the usual interval. Seasonal heat pump maintenance covers those checks, and City & Suburban maintenance plans include seasonal check-ups.

Can a geothermal heat pump be repaired like an air-source one?

Largely, yes. A geothermal heat pump shares the core parts of an air-source unit: compressor, blower, reversing valve, controls and a refrigerant circuit. Faults in those parts are found and fixed much the same way. The buried ground loop is different. Loop leaks, flushing and drilling are specialist work, so the loop needs its own plan. Indoor units commonly last 20 years or more, and ground loops far longer, which makes repair the usual answer when one indoor part fails. City & Suburban repairs all makes and models and diagnoses before quoting; the household should call to confirm a geothermal unit before booking.

Why does a heat pump act up after a new smart thermostat?

Heat pump thermostats need settings that furnace thermostats do not. The O/B setting tells the reversing valve which mode means heating, and the wrong choice runs the system backward. Aux heat staging and outdoor lockout temperatures decide when the backup heat strips step in. A smart thermostat set up with furnace settings often blows cool air on heat or leans hard on aux heat. The technician checks thermostat wiring and setup before any part is ordered, since fixing a setting costs far less than replacing a part that was never broken.
